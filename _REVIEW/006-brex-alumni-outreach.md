# 006 Brex CMU Alumni Outreach (LinkedIn DMs)

**Goal:** Surface a CMU alum at Brex willing to either (a) submit an internal referral on your behalf, OR (b) give you a warm intro to a recruiter, OR (c) share an informational chat about the team.

---

## Step 1, find Brex CMU alumni

### LinkedIn searches (run in order, broad to narrow)

1. `Brex AND "Carnegie Mellon"` — broadest
2. `Brex AND "Carnegie Mellon" AND engineer`
3. `Brex AND "Carnegie Mellon" AND ("software engineer" OR SWE OR SDE)` — most targeted
4. `Brex AND "CMU MISM"` — Heinz alumni specifically (smaller pool but most relevant)

**Filter:** keep "currently at Brex" only. Skip "ex-Brex" people.

### Backup paths

- **CMU Alumni Online Community**: [community.cmu.edu/s/](https://community.cmu.edu/s/) — search "Brex"
- **Tartan Connect** alumni mentorship platform (linked from main CMU careers site)

## What to do if they say yes

### If they offer an internal referral

1. Send them: full name, email, the role link, your resume PDF
2. Ask: "Would you like me to draft a 1-paragraph blurb you can include in the referral?" (saves them effort, makes referral stronger)
3. After they submit the referral, you're now in Brex's internal referral pool. **Put their name in the application's referral field if you haven't applied yet.** If you already applied without the referral, message Brex's recruiting team to connect the two records.

### If they offer a warm intro (no formal referral)

1. Ask them to copy the recruiter on an email (better than just LinkedIn DM)
2. Don't put their name in the referral field; leave it blank
3. Mention them naturally in the recruiter conversation: "I was introduced to you by [Name] at Brex"

### If they offer an informational chat only

1. Schedule the call
2. Come prepared with specific questions (see "Conversation prep" below)
3. After the chat, send a thank-you note. **Don't immediately ask for a referral**, that's pushy. Wait 1-2 weeks; if rapport built, ask then.

---

## Conversation prep, what to ask if they say yes to a chat

Public Brex engineering intel (use to ground questions):

- **Stack**: Python, Go, TypeScript; AWS + Kubernetes; PostgreSQL ledger
- **Engineering blog**: [brex.com/journal/category/engineering](https://www.brex.com/journal/category/engineering)
- **Values**: production-first, ledger correctness, "the unsexy work matters"
- **Hiring loop**: typically 1 phone screen (technical) → 1 take-home or coding interview → 1-2 rounds system design + behavioral

### Questions to ask

1. "What does the day-to-day look like for an SWE intern at Brex?"
2. "What's the biggest production-engineering challenge the team is facing right now?"
3. "What separates strong applicants from weak ones in your view?"
4. "What's the on-call expectation, even for interns?" (signals you take production seriously)
5. "Are there specific projects you'd hope an intern could ship by end of summer?"

### Avoid

- Comp questions in the first conversation, even if you're curious
- "What's it like working at Brex?" (too vague, signals lack of prep)
- Questions answered by their public materials (do your homework)

---

## Sending tactics

1. **Don't attach resume in DM** — triggers spam filters. Offer to send via email.
2. **Send 3-5 messages**, not 50. CMU-to-CMU response rate ~25-30%; expect 1-2 useful replies.
3. **Connection request handling**: if LinkedIn requires connection first, customize the connect note (same content, trimmed to fit ~270 char limit).
4. **Wait 7-10 days before any follow-up**. One polite follow-up max, then move on.
5. **Update tracker**: in `data/applications.md` Notes for Brex, append `A2 sent 2026-04-XX to [Name]; replied [date] with [outcome]`.

## Don't include in messages

- No em-dashes
- No CPT/visa explainer
- No "passionate about" / "leveraged" / "results-oriented" boilerplate
- Don't pitch yourself as a perfect candidate; pitch yourself as a useful one
